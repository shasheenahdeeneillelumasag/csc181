### Selected problem

MSU-IIT students have no organized way to buy and sell secondhand school materials. Senior students cannot easily sell unused school materials because their posts get buried in Facebook groups, while underclassmen spend considerable time searching through multiple groups for affordable options.

### Selection rationale

The team selected the secondhand school materials problem because it is one we experience directly as MSU-IIT students, which gives us easy access to sellers and buyers for interviews. It can be built within one semester using the team’s basic web development skills, and its success can be measured through a timed search test and a listing-posting test. We did not choose the city hall guide because it depends on city hall staff to verify requirements that change over time, and the team does not experience that problem as directly. We did not choose the study room tracker because reliable occupancy data would need continuous staff updates or hardware sensors, which are outside our scope. 

## 3. Preliminary Project Proposal

### Working project title

MSU-IIT Preloved Marketplace

### Problem statement

MSU-IIT students have no organized way to buy and sell secondhand school materials. Senior students are left with unused school materials such as textbooks, lab gowns, and drafting tools that they cannot easily sell because their posts get buried in Facebook groups. Underclassmen, who often need these items for only one semester, spend considerable time searching through multiple Facebook groups for affordable options. They also waste time messaging sellers, only to learn the item was already sold, because posts do not show availability. As a result, seniors may not recover the cost of unused items, underclassmen may overspend, and both groups lose time. A better approach would let students find and list items in one searchable place and see what is still available. We assume sellers will list and buyers will switch; both still need validation. 

### Proposed solution

The team proposes a lightweight web application where MSU-IIT students can buy and sell secondhand school materials in one organized, searchable place instead of scattered Facebook group posts. Students register and log in using their official @g.msuiit.edu.ph email address. Sellers post a listing with a photo, title, category, course, price, and condition, and buyers can browse or search listings by title, course, or category. Logged-in buyers contact sellers through the Facebook link or mobile number provided on the listing, and sellers are notified when posting that these details will be shown to other MSU-IIT students. Once an item is sold, the seller marks it as sold, which removes it from search results and deletes its contact details so buyers no longer message about items that are gone. Payment and meetups are arranged in person on campus. To avoid an empty platform, the prototype will be pre-loaded with real listings from willing students.

## 5. Initial Scope

**Intended users** 
1. Student Buyers:  
* Underclassmen and freshmen looking to buy required, semester-bound academic materials at student-friendly prices.  
* Students seeking an efficient, organized way to search and filter listings by course or subject rather than endlessly scrolling through unindexed social media groups.  
2. Student Sellers:  
* Upperclassmen, seniors, and graduating students (or any student) who have finished their courses and have unused learning materials to sell.  
* Students looking for a direct platform to reach fellow MSU-IIT students and immediately mark items as sold to avoid repetitive and outdated inquiries. 

**Setting**
* Digital context: A lightweight, responsive web application accessible on desktop and mobile browsers, designed to load quickly on MSU-IIT Wi-Fi and mobile data.  
* Physical context: The MSU-IIT campus, where buyers and sellers arrange payment and pickup in person.

### In-scope capabilities

1. **Register and log in** using an official @g.msuiit.edu.ph email address.  
2. **Post a listing** with a photo, title, category, course, price, and condition (chosen from a short list).  
3. **Browse and search listings** by title, course, or category.  
4. **Contact a seller** through the Facebook link or mobile number they entered on their listing, visible only to logged-in users. A notice at posting time tells the seller that these details will be shown to other MSU-IIT students.  
5. **Mark a listing as sold** so it no longer appears in search results. Sold listings have their contact details removed.

### Out-of-scope capabilities

1. **In-app chat or messaging.** Buyers and sellers contact each other through the Facebook link or mobile number on the listing.  
2. **Online payments (GCash, card, escrow).** Price and payment are agreed and settled in person.  
3. **Delivery or shipping.** Meetups happen on campus.  
4. **Seller ratings, reviews, or reporting.** Ratings require verified transactions, which the system doesn’t handle.  
5. **Verification of enrollment against the official MSU-IIT student database.** Access is limited to @g.msuiit.edu.ph email accounts only.

### Expected deliverables

1. **Working web prototype** with the five in-scope capabilities, pre-loaded with real listings from willing students.  
2. **Documentation:** the final project proposal, a user guide covering how to post and search, and a short technical overview of the stack and data collected.  
3. **Testing evidence:** results of the timed search test (about five users) and the observed listing-posting test, including steps taken, errors, and whether help was needed.  
4. **Source code** in the team repository.

## 6. Review and Next Steps

### Change made after reviewing the proposal

* Before review: The initial scope was a campus marketplace where students could list items, browse and search, and contact sellers. It did not define how buyers and sellers would communicate, how payment would be handled, or how the platform would build trust between users.

* After review: The scope is now defined as five capabilities: registering with an official @g.msuiit.edu.ph email, posting a listing, browsing and searching, contacting a seller, and marking a listing as sold. Contact is made through a Facebook link or mobile number that the seller chooses to provide, visible only to logged-in users. Payment and meetups are settled in person on campus. In-app messaging, online payments, delivery, and seller ratings are excluded from this semester's version.  
* Reason: These exclusions keep the project achievable within one semester and consistent with the Data Privacy Act (RA 10173), since the system would not need to store private conversations, handle financial data, or collect reviews about identifiable students. Using contact details voluntarily shared by the seller also matches how students already communicate through Messenger. Adding the "mark as sold" capability addresses a gap identified in the problem statement: students contacting sellers about items that are no longer available.

### Biggest remaining risk

The biggest remaining risk is that students may not switch from Facebook groups to the new platform. The marketplace is only useful if enough seniors list their unused materials and enough underclassmen search the platform instead of scrolling through groups. If either side does not participate, the other side has no reason to use it. The team has not yet confirmed that seniors are willing to list items on a new platform or that underclassmen will search instead of using Facebook. Pre-loading the pilot with real listings from willing students will reduce this risk, but it will not guarantee continued use.

### Three unanswered questions

1. How many seniors have unused materials they would sell, and how many students look for secondhand items each semester?  
2. What do students like about posting and buying in Facebook groups now, and what would convince them to use a separate searchable platform?  
3. Will students be comfortable showing their Facebook link or mobile number to other students, and will sellers remember to mark items as sold?
