### Selected problem

Newcomers and first-time applicants in Iligan City who need services at the Iligan City Hall, such as first-time starters of a new business, first-time job applicants, and people seeking a particular service for the first time, are unable to identify which office can best address their concern, which papers they need to submit, and what the next step in the process is. Because this information is not presented in one clear, easy-to-follow place, they wait in line, get referred to other offices, and return repeatedly for requirements they had forgotten.

### Selection rationale

We selected the Iligan City Hall services problem because it affects a clearly identifiable group, newcomers and first-time applicants, who lose time to long waits, referrals between offices, and repeat trips for missing paperwork. A single, easy-to-follow guide to offices, requirements, and steps would give them a clear benefit. We can reach likely stakeholders through short interviews or surveys, and the requirements are published in public documents such as the Citizen's Charter, which we plan to ask staff to verify. A read-only web guide for a limited set of services needs only basic web skills and free tools, and because it collects no personal data it can be demonstrated safely. We did not choose the study-space tracker because live occupancy needs constant manual updates or sensors, nor the secondhand marketplace because it depends on building an active user base and managing accounts.

## 3. Preliminary Project Proposal

### Working project title

Iligan City Hall Services Requirements Navigator

### Problem statement

Newcomers and first-time applicants in Iligan City experience confusion when they need services at city hall, such as business permits, building permits, traffic clearances, and birth or marriage certificates. They struggle most when they arrive and cannot tell which office handles their concern, which documents to bring, or what step comes next. Currently, they ask employees or other visitors, follow posted instructions that may be outdated, or move between counters until someone points them in the right direction. This may lead to long waits, being referred from one office to another, and repeated trips for missing paperwork. The project aims to support clear, easy to find guidance on offices, requirements, and steps within the selected city hall services. The team still needs to confirm that visitors have a smartphone and mobile data, that requirements stay mostly the same, and that city hall staff are willing to verify the steps.

### Proposed solution

The Iligan City Hall Services Requirements Navigator is an informational system that lets residents look up a city hall service and see the office to visit, the documents to bring, and the steps to follow in order. It covers only the selected services, collects no personal data, and displays a "Last Updated" date on every page so users know how recent the information is. By putting this guidance in one place before users arrive, it reduces confusion, wasted trips, and referrals between offices.

## 5. Initial Scope

### Intended users and setting

**Intended users**

1. Primary users: Iligan City residents and visitors, or people who need to process documents or services at the City Hall of Iligan City.
2. Secondary users / indirect beneficiaries: The clerks or front desk employees, who may refer visitors to the guide but will not use or edit it.

**Setting**

1. Physical setting
   - At home: Users checking the list of requirements on the app using their phones or PCs.
   - On-site (Iligan City Hall): Users standing in the lobby, waiting sheds, hallways, benches, or on the lines outside the service window, rechecking the document lists.
2. Digital setting
   - Mobile-first browser: Mobile browsers like Google Chrome, Brave, etc.
   - Network conditions: Many users may rely on prepaid mobile data (assumption, to be checked).

### In-scope capabilities

1. Search for a city hall service by name, or browse services by category (business, building and property, traffic, civil registry, and cedula).
2. View the documents required for a selected service and where each one is obtained.
3. View the steps to follow in order for a selected service, including which office to visit at each step.
4. View an office's address and its location on a map, and open it in a map app for directions.
5. Initial services covered (to be finalized from the Citizen's Charter and staff input): business permit (new and renewal), building permit, traffic clearance, birth and marriage certificates, and community tax certificate (cedula).

### Out-of-scope capabilities

1. Applying for, submitting, or paying for any permit or certificate online. The app is an informational guide only.
2. User accounts, logins, ID or document uploads, and saving or tracking user progress. This keeps the app free of personal data under RA 10173.
3. Queue numbers, appointment booking, or any connection to city hall's live systems.
4. Fees, taxes, and processing times. The business permit fee depends on the type of business and gross receipts, so showing it would add a lot of data and risk errors.
5. In-app turn-by-turn navigation, live location tracking, and indoor floor plans. Directions open in the user's own map app, and live location would be personal data.
6. An admin or content-editing interface for city hall staff. Content will be updated by the team in the data files.

### Expected deliverables

1. A working web prototype of the Iligan City Hall Services Requirements Navigator. Users can search or browse the selected services, see the required documents, the steps and offices in order, and each office's address and map location.
2. A service information set covering the selected services. It is based on official sources such as the Iligan City Citizen's Charter, checked by city hall staff where possible, and each page shows a "Last Updated" date.
3. Brief documentation: a short guide on how to use the prototype, plus the list of services covered and their sources.
4. Testing evidence: the results of timing at least 5 users on a task, and a comparison of the app's content with the official guide, matching our two success indicators.

Where no official data is available, we will use placeholder data clearly labeled as sample data. |

## 6. Review and Next Steps

### Change made after reviewing the proposal

- Before review: The idea covered city hall services in general, including any business permit or local certificate, without a defined limit on the offices or services included.
- After review: The scope is limited to the services the team believes, from personal experience and the Citizen's Charter, are commonly needed and involve multiple steps (to be confirmed with city hall staff). These are grouped into five categories: business and licensing, building and property permits, traffic-related services, civil registry documents, and the community tax certificate (cedula). For each service, the web-based guide shows the required documents, the steps and offices involved, and the office locations.
- Reason: Covering every city hall service was too vague to define and too large to verify and complete within the semester, since the Iligan City Citizen's Charter alone lists services from 23 offices. The team therefore selected the services it believes, from personal experience and the Citizen's Charter, are commonly needed and involve multiple steps. Focusing on these makes the project clearer and achievable within the semester, lets the team verify the information with city hall staff, and directs the guide to where users most need help.

### Biggest remaining risk

The biggest remaining risk is the accuracy and upkeep of the service information. The team has no access to city hall's private records and depends on public documents such as the Iligan City Citizen's Charter, along with city hall staff who have not yet agreed to verify the steps. If the requirements change, or if staff do not confirm that the guide matches what actually happens at the counters, the app could send users to the wrong office or tell them to bring incomplete papers. That would repeat the very problem the project is meant to solve and would undermine the success indicator on content accuracy. To reduce this risk, the team will build the guide from the Citizen's Charter first, show a "Last Updated" date and the source on every page, label any unverified item as sample data, and ask the information desk early to check a printed sample so the team learns whether staff will cooperate before the full data set is built.

### Three unanswered questions

1. How will we find and approach first-time visitors at City Hall to test our prototype and get their feedback?
2. Should we write the prototype instructions in English, Tagalog, or Bisaya so first-time applicants can read them most easily?
3. What is the best way to ask a City Hall clerk to review our documents list without disturbing them during work hours?
